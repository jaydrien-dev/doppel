# Doppel — Future Product Ideas

Doppel is an information marketplace. Anyone with deep knowledge in any domain — medicine, law, cooking, fitness, music, history, engineering, language, design, science — can create a clone. Anyone with a specialist question can access it. These ideas are assessed against that premise, not a business-tools lens.

Game-changing test: does it change the category of value delivered, is it only possible with a specific person's knowledge (not generic AI), and does it make something previously inaccessible accessible?

---

## 1. Voice — The Clone You Can Actually Call

Consumer talks to the clone out loud. No typing, no screen. The clone speaks back in the creator's voice. A chef walks you through a technique while your hands are occupied. A fitness trainer coaches you through a workout. A therapist-trained clone is available at 3am when you can't sleep and need to talk something through. A language teacher holds a conversation with you in the language you're learning.

**Why game-changing:** most domains where specialist knowledge matters are not screen-native. Cooking, working out, driving, building, practising — these happen away from a keyboard. Voice makes the clone available in the moment the knowledge is actually needed, not before or after. ElevenLabs already in the stack. The backend doesn't change.

---

## 2. The Clone Onboards You — It Interviews You First

First session isn't the consumer asking questions. The clone interviews them. What do you already know? What are you trying to do? What have you tried? Where are you stuck? It builds a model of who this person is before ever giving an answer — their level, their context, their specific situation.

**Why game-changing:** a chef's clone advising someone who has never cooked is different from advising a culinary student. A doctor's clone talking to someone anxious about a diagnosis is different from talking to a medical professional. Generic AI has no idea which one you are. This clone does — and every answer it gives from that point is calibrated to you specifically.

---

## 3. The Clone Trains You — Structured Learning from a Real Expert

Not Q&A. A curriculum. The clone knows your level (from onboarding), knows what you need to learn next, and teaches — explains concepts, gives you exercises, corrects your work, adapts as you improve. The creator's specific way of understanding their domain, taught in their voice, calibrated to your gaps.

A musician's clone teaches you theory the way that musician thinks about it. A historian's clone teaches you to read primary sources the way they do. A scientist's clone walks you through how to actually think about experimental design.

**Why game-changing:** the best learning happens from people with a real point of view, not from curricula designed to offend no one. Every domain has people who think about it in a way that changes how you see it. That's been inaccessible unless you knew them personally. This makes it accessible to anyone.

---

## 4. Two Clones Debate — Preserved Disagreement

Two clones with genuinely different knowledge or perspectives argue a specific question in front of the consumer. Not synthesised into mush. The disagreement preserved. Consumer watches, asks follow-ups, probes the tension.

Two doctors with different treatment philosophies on a controversial diagnosis. Two historians with different interpretations of the same event. Two nutritionists on a contested dietary question. A traditional chef and a modernist chef on technique. A classically trained musician and a self-taught one on how to learn.

**Why game-changing:** the most useful information in any domain is often in the disagreements between experts, not the consensus. Consensus is the thing everyone already knows. Disagreement is where the real signal is. No other product preserves actual intellectual tension between two real people's knowledge. This is what makes the marketplace — not individual clones, but the friction between them.

---

## 5. The Right Expert for the Exact Question

Some questions only make sense to one specific type of person. A rare autoimmune condition that only a specialist in that exact area has seen. A legal question specific to one jurisdiction and one industry. A vintage instrument that only someone who has worked on that model understands. A dialect that only a native linguist can parse.

The marketplace makes it possible to find the exact clone for the exact question — not the most famous person in a broad category, but the person whose specific depth matches the specific need. The clone's profile surfaces what it actually knows with high confidence. Consumer is matched to it.

**Why game-changing:** this is the core promise of the marketplace at full resolution. Generic AI gives a plausible answer to anything. This gives the right answer from the right person. The difference matters enormously for high-stakes, highly specific questions — the kind where being wrong has real consequences.

---

## 6. The Introduction — Access to the Real Person

When the clone identifies that a consumer's situation genuinely requires a real conversation — the question is too specific, the stakes are too high, the context is too unusual — it facilitates a warm introduction to the creator. Pre-qualified, context-rich, both parties informed.

The clone tells the creator: "this person has a situation I can't fully address — here's why, here's the context." Creator decides whether to engage. Consumer gets access they would never otherwise have found.

**Why game-changing:** the most valuable thing in any knowledge domain is access to the right person directly. The clone doesn't try to replace that — it earns it. It becomes a quality filter that makes rare access more reachable for consumers and less noisy for creators. Doppel earns on the match.

---

## 7. Persistent Mental Model — The Clone Knows Where You Go Wrong

Over time the clone builds a model of this specific consumer: how they tend to reason, where they consistently make the same mistake, what they underweight, what they overcomplicate. It starts correcting for those patterns without being asked.

A student always skips foundational steps. A home cook always under-seasons. A language learner always avoids the subjunctive. A researcher always overfits to their prior. The clone notices, names it, holds it across sessions.

**Why game-changing:** this is what the best teachers and mentors do — they don't just answer questions, they know where your thinking breaks down. That's been available only to people lucky enough to have that relationship. This makes it available to anyone, across any domain, and it compounds — the longer you use it, the more precisely it models you.

---

## 8. Live Context — The Clone in the Moment

Via the desktop overlay or mobile: consumer is doing something right now and needs specialist knowledge in the moment. Not prep, not debrief — during. A home mechanic under a car who needs to know what they're looking at. A patient in a doctor's office who wants to know what question to ask next. Someone at a market who needs to know if a piece of vintage furniture is authentic. Someone mid-conversation who needs to know how to say something in another language.

The clone sees what the consumer sees (screenshot / camera / typed description) and responds in real time.

**Why game-changing:** knowledge gaps don't happen at desks. They happen in the world, in the middle of doing things. The overlay makes the clone available at the exact moment the information is needed, in context, rather than requiring the consumer to interrupt what they're doing and go look something up.

---

## 9. Community Convergence — What Everyone Is Asking

When many consumers ask a clone similar questions, patterns surface. "The 200 people who asked this clone about X — here's where they agreed, here's where they diverged, here's the question that kept coming up that the clone flagged as genuinely contested."

The marketplace produces aggregate intelligence from individual queries. Not just "this is what the clone thinks" — "this is what the clone thinks, and here's what 200 people who came to it with the same problem found most useful."

**Why game-changing:** the information marketplace doesn't just connect individuals to experts — it accumulates knowledge across those connections. That accumulated signal doesn't exist anywhere else. It's only possible because of the volume of specific questions flowing through specific clones.

---

## 10. Clone as Long-Term Companion for a Domain

Not a one-off query. A relationship. Consumer is learning to cook — they have a chef's clone as their ongoing cooking companion for a year. Learning a language — the language teacher's clone is who they practise with three times a week. Managing a health condition — a specialist's clone tracks their situation over months.

The clone accumulates context about where the consumer started, how they've progressed, what has worked, what hasn't. Answers shift as the consumer grows. The clone celebrates progress, notices regression, adapts.

**Why game-changing:** the transactional model (question → answer) captures a fraction of what's possible. The compounding model (relationship → growth over time) is what actually changes people. That's what the best teachers, coaches, and advisors deliver. This makes it available at scale across any domain.

---

## Desktop Overlay — Delivery Mechanism for #5 and #8

The overlay is not a feature — it's the infrastructure that makes real-time, live-context, in-the-moment access possible. Screen capture, voice input, always-available sidebar. See previous spec. Build the shell first; layer capabilities on top.

## What Was Cut and Why

- **Adversarial / red-team mode** — framed around business pitches and plans. The underlying idea (expert challenges your thinking) is real but it's captured by the training and debate features with a broader lens.
- **Rehearsal mode (plays the other party)** — too specifically about professional negotiations and pitches.
- **Real-time meeting co-pilot** — too specifically about professional meetings.
- **Clone-authored deliverable** — good feature but the "deliverable" framing skews business. The training and companion features cover the value more broadly.
- **Shareable links / bookmarks / notifications** — growth mechanics.
- **Async brief delivery** — email delivery of a chat answer is not a new interaction model.
- **Curated feed** — content curation is commoditised.
- **Public Q&A wall** — SEO play, not consumer value.
